# Academic and exchange evidence on 0DTE profitability and intraday predictability (as of October 2026)

Method note: Full text was read for three primary sources (Beckmeyer-Branger-Gayda Dec 2023 version; Baltussen-Da-Lammers-Martens JFE 2021; Zarattini-Aziz-Barbon Feb 2025 version). Everything else comes from abstracts, seminar pages or secondary summaries, and is labelled as such. SSRN returned HTTP 403 for every direct fetch. Items that could not be checked against a primary text are marked "secondary" or placed in Gaps.

Calendar context for all 0DTE findings: SPX had Mon/Wed/Fri expiries from 2016, Tue/Thu were added on 2022-05-16, so SPX 0DTE exists every weekday only from mid-May 2022. Retail identification in Beckmeyer et al. starts Feb 2021. Anything before 2022 for "daily 0DTE" is a Mon/Wed/Fri (and earlier, Friday-only) sample. SPY and QQQ have had daily expirations for longer, but almost none of the 0DTE academic work covers SPY/QQQ options. It is SPX/SPXW only. Nothing below is evidence about SPY/QQQ option microstructure; spreads and tick sizes differ.

## Q1. What do the main 0DTE papers find about retail profitability, who loses, how much is paid in spreads, and do buyers or sellers earn the premium?

### Takeaway
Retail traders in aggregate lose money on SPX 0DTE, mostly through transaction costs on long (debit, single-leg) positions. Short/credit and multi-leg positions are on average profitable even after fees, which is consistent with sellers collecting a variance risk premium. Retail is net long, so the aggregate is negative.

### Cited Findings

**Beckmeyer, Branger, Gayda (Univ. of Münster), "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?" (first version 30 Mar 2023; Dec 15 2023 version read in full; working paper, no journal publication found)**
- Data: Cboe SPX transaction-level data (complete SPX coverage because SPX trades only on Cboe). Retail trades are identified via the Automated Price Improvement Mechanism codes (SLAN single-leg, MLAT multi-leg), only available from Feb 2021, and only for orders up to 10 contracts. Sample Jan 2021 to Sep 2023. Trade direction by quote rule. — [Dec 2023 version PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- More than 75% of retail S&P 500 option trades are 0DTE; more than 40% of all traded SPX contracts expire the same day. Retail is only about 2-4% of total SPX option volume, but exceeds 6% in 0DTE. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Aggregate retail loss: $241,000 per average day over Feb 2021 to Sep 2023. Since the daily-expiry calendar began on 16 May 2022: $350,000 per day. Cumulative net-of-fee losses exceed $125 million by Sep 2023. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf). The March 2023 version reported $184,000 (full sample) and $358,000 (post May 2022) per day, so the numbers moved between versions (secondary: [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634), [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance)).
- Gross vs. net: Using mid-quote as the price, cumulative gross loss is about -$30 million by end of sample; using actual transaction prices the net loss exceeds -$125 million. More than $90 million of losses are attributed to transaction costs (spread paid to the market maker), the rest to positioning. Gross cumulative P&L was flat after Nov 2022 while net kept falling, i.e. spread cost is the persistent drag. These figures exclude broker commissions and regulatory/clearing fees (Robinhood: about $0.01 regulatory + $0.02 clearing per contract at time of writing), so they are conservative. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Roughly 60% of daily losses come from transaction costs; roughly 60% come from 0DTE put exposure; retail buys perform particularly poorly. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Spreads: Retail (price-improved) effective spreads are 6.0% for calls and 5.0% for puts versus 12.5% and 9.6% for non-retail trades, "a considerable improvement of roughly 50%". 18% of retail trades execute at the mid-quote. 74.2% of retail orders are for a single contract; 23.5% for 2-5 contracts. Retail and non-retail trade similar moneyness (ATM and slightly OTM) and 3-24 hours to maturity. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Buyers vs. sellers (key result): Long positions lose, short positions are profitable even after fees. Over the full sample, retail lost $364,000 per average day on debit orders (net of costs) and made $122,000 per day on credit orders. Authors read this as some retail investors harvesting the variance risk premium (citing Coval-Shumway). Because retail is net long, aggregate P&L is negative both gross and net. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Time-of-day: net and gross profits are negative in every trading hour of the expiry day; buys lose and sells make money in each hour. The hourly table (extracted from the PDF layout; units and scaling unverified) shows net profit at 9:30 -0.46, 10:00 -0.50, 11:00 -0.31, 12:00 -0.22, 13:00 -0.36, 14:00 -0.28, 15:00 -0.30. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Day-of-week: Net profits negative Monday to Friday (example: -$429,000 on the average Tuesday), though retail buys were slightly positive after fees on the average Thursday. Monthly variation is large: +$286,000/day in Jan 2022 versus -$680,000/day in Sep 2022; several months lose more than $500,000/day. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Strategy-level, margin-adjusted returns: single-leg trades lose on average (puts -5.6%, calls -9.4% of posted margin; medians about -0.7% and -0.8%, only 25% of trades earn more than +0.6%). Median put spread +3.0% and call spread +3.3% margin-adjusted. Multi-leg trades earn on average 0.17% more than single-leg. Many multi-leg positions are profitable, but profits are "overshadowed by a few but very significant outliers." — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Trade-level drivers: one standard deviation more Gamma (Vega) in a trade raises return by 0.019%-0.045% (0.018%-0.094%) over the rest of the day, i.e. retail is compensated for bearing jump/vol risk. Trades with high implied vol and trades requiring an upfront payment earn significantly negative returns. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Positioning: end customers buy more 0DTE than they sell, leaving market makers net short 0DTE in aggregate (growing in recent months of the sample). — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Authors note Cboe and SIFMA actively lobbied for retail-targeted changes (AIM auction for SPX, proposal SR-CBOE-2020-051) and conclude the price-improvement benefit only partially offsets retail's costs. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)

**Bryzgalova, Pavlova, Sikorskaya, "Retail Trading in Options and the Rise of the Big Three Wholesalers," Journal of Finance 78(6), 2023 (CEPR DP17688 Nov 2022)** — abstract-level only; not 0DTE specific (single-name and ETF options)
- Retail is over 60% of total options volume (by their measure); about 90% of PFOF comes from three wholesalers; retail favors cheap weekly options with an average bid-ask spread of 12.6% and loses money on average. — [SUFE summary of abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/359005) (secondary; I could not open the paper PDF)

**Vilkov, "0DTE Trading Rules" (SSRN 4641356, Nov 2023; a later version reportedly titled "...Tail Risk, Implementation, and Tactical Timing", 2026)** — secondary summaries only
- Tests unhedged, cash-settled SPX 0DTE static rules (ATM straddles, strangles, call/put spreads, ratio spreads, risk reversals) opened at 10:00 (also 13:00 and 15:00) and held to the 16:00 close. Sample Sep 2016 to Jan 2024 (the CXO summary of an earlier draft says through May 2023). — [Hull Tactical summary](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/), [CXO Advisory summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/0dte-strategy-outcomes)
- Reported: a variance risk premium exists (implied vol tends to exceed subsequently realized vol), some strategies look profitable at the median (buying deep ITM calls; selling OTM calls and puts), but return distributions are very wide and skewed, average returns of most strategies are statistically insignificant, and much of P&L is driven by realized skewness of the index. The premium is small relative to the width of the distribution. — [Hull Tactical](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/)
- One newsletter quotes a 10:00-to-close premium of about 0.001 (unit unclear) that transaction costs largely absorb — [Breaking Nasdaq newsletter](https://breakingnasdaq.beehiiv.com/p/the-0dte-illusion) (low-quality secondary source; treat as unverified).
- A third-party replication page describes the 2026 version as covering SPXW Sep 2016 to Jan 2026, finding unconditional 0DTE weak after costs and conditional out-of-sample rules on selected strategies reaching Sharpe 1.0-1.3 — [TomeVault](https://tomevault.io/tome/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies) (the page returned HTTP 503 when I tried to open it; the claim comes only from a search snippet, so unverified).

**Hizmeri et al., "0DTE Asset Pricing" (SSRN 4701401; seminar abstract Feb 2025)**
- Intraday equity premium comes mostly from market returns between -5% and 0%; investors demand a high premium for variance risk within the day, mainly for upside risk; the variance risk premium negatively predicts intraday market returns (driven by the upside component); 0DTE options violate stochastic dominance and exploiting this mispricing is "highly profitable" (abstract claim; no numbers, costs not stated). — [Univ. of Liverpool seminar page](http://www.liverpool.ac.uk/redbrick/institute-for-financial-and-actuarial-mathematics/past-seminars/rodrigo-hizmeri/)

**Bandi, Fusari, Renò, "0DTE Option Pricing" (SSRN 4503344, 2023)**
- A pricing model using an Edgeworth-type expansion with time-varying skewness (leverage) and kurtosis (vol-of-vol), claimed to improve pricing versus benchmark models, and gives "suggestive results" on near-instantaneous predictability from estimating 0DTE return and variance risk premia. I found no quantitative premium estimates in the accessible text. — [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/0dte-option-pricing-bandi-federico/10014348685), [Johns Hopkins Carey summary](https://carey.jhu.edu/news/risk-reward-insights-0dte-option-trading)

### Inferences
- The retail result is a cost-and-direction result, not a "retail picks the wrong direction" result. Gross (mid-quote) retail P&L is only moderately negative (about -$30M over about 2.6 years); the net loss is about four times larger. Spread cost is the main channel, so for a retail developer the actionable lever is execution (limit orders at or inside mid, spreads over naked singles), not signal quality.
- Because the same paper shows credit positions making money after fees and debit positions losing, the evidence is consistent with a positive (seller-collected) 0DTE variance risk premium. But it is the premium net of retail price improvement; non-retail and institutional sellers, who pay about 2x the effective spread, are not in these P&L numbers.
- Aggregate loss of $350k/day spread over thousands of retail participants is a small per-trade amount: this is a cost drag, not a catastrophe-level finding, and it does not by itself show a strategy family is unprofitable for a disciplined individual.
- None of this tests SPY or QQQ options, which have penny-wide spreads on ATM near-expiry strikes; the 5-6% effective spread figure should not be assumed to apply.

### Gaps
- No primary-text access to Dim-Eraker-Vilkov, Vilkov, Bandi-Fusari-Renò, Hizmeri, or Bryzgalova numbers beyond abstracts (SSRN blocked); no verified statistical significance for the Vilkov strategies.
- No academic paper found measuring retail 0DTE P&L after Sep 2023 (2024-2026). Beckmeyer et al. is the only retail P&L study located; no later published update was found, so the sample is stale by about 3 years.
- No peer-reviewed evidence on SPY/QQQ 0DTE option profitability for retail.
- No Federal Reserve, BIS or ECB note on 0DTE profitability was found (one search for FEDS notes returned nothing relevant).

## Q2. Is the 0DTE variance/volatility risk premium positive (sellers earn), and how does it vary by time of day, event days, moneyness?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to a positive but small and fat-tailed 0DTE variance risk premium (sellers earn on average), concentrated in tail-heavy payoffs and largely consumed by costs on static strategies. Time-of-day and event-day patterns are poorly documented in accessible sources; what I could verify is limited.

### Cited Findings
- Retail credit orders earned +$122,000/day after fees while debit orders lost $364,000/day (Feb 2021 to Sep 2023, SPX). — [Beckmeyer et al.](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Beckmeyer et al. build a "VIX0DTE" expensiveness measure from option trades 9:30-9:40 and report that SPX 0DTE options became drastically more expensive after Jan 2022; retail net profits were negatively related to this expensiveness over the full sample (more expensive = bigger retail losses), but no such relationship after daily expirations began in May 2022 (they read this as retail learning, "indicative"). — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Dim, Eraker, Vilkov report that 0DTE options have the largest volatility risk premium among maturities (secondary summary of the paper). — [Harbourfront Quant summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/impact-of-zero-dte-options-on-the) (secondary Substack; not verified against the paper)
- Vilkov: VRP (implied minus realized variance while the position is open) is present but modest versus outcome dispersion; P&L is dominated by realized skewness. — [Hull Tactical](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/)
- Hizmeri et al.: intraday VRP is high and mainly compensation for upside (not downside) risk. — [Liverpool seminar abstract](http://www.liverpool.ac.uk/redbrick/institute-for-financial-and-actuarial-mathematics/past-seminars/rodrigo-hizmeri/)
- Moneyness/structure: retail high-IV trades lose; 0DTE puts account for about 60% of retail daily losses; credit spreads (median +3.0% put / +3.3% call margin-adjusted) outperform single legs. — [Beckmeyer et al.](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Event days: Londono and Samadi use 0DTE options to study pricing of uncertainty around economic releases (cited in Beckmeyer et al.; I did not read the paper). — [Beckmeyer et al.](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf). OptionMetrics notes a volume surge right after the 2 pm FOMC announcement (descriptive, not about premium) — [OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/news/record-0dte-options-volumes-signal-shifting-market-dynamics/) (search snippet only)

### Inferences
- A seller's edge on SPX 0DTE is plausible but is a tail-risk-compensation premium: median outcomes look good, means are insignificant (Vilkov), and the Beckmeyer data show monthly sign flips in aggregate retail P&L (+$286k to -$680k per day). A retail seller needs defined risk and explicit tail-event budgeting.
- Time-of-day: the only quantitative time-of-day result found shows buyers losing and sellers gaining in every hour on the expiry day, with no hour where buying is profitable. That argues against "buy late-day lottos" but hourly premium magnitude by moneyness is unmeasured in what I could access.

### Gaps
- No accessible source quantifies 0DTE VRP by hour, by FOMC/CPI day versus normal day, or by moneyness bucket. These are probably in the Dim-Eraker-Vilkov, Vilkov and Bandi-Fusari-Renò full texts (SSRN blocked).
- No numbers for Dim-Eraker-Vilkov's VRP, and no statistical significance for any 0DTE VRP estimate.
- No post-2023 realized-sample evidence on whether the 0DTE VRP has compressed as volume (now over 60% of SPX options) has risen.

## Q3. What does Cboe's own research say (volume, retail share, buyer/seller P&L)? Flag where Cboe is an interested party.

### Takeaway
Cboe publishes volume and composition statistics (0DTE share of SPX volume, retail share, defined-risk share) but, in what I could access, no buyer/seller P&L. Cboe has a direct commercial interest (it exclusively lists SPX options) and its research is framed to counter "0DTE is dangerous/gambling" narratives. Use its volume statistics, discount its interpretation.

### Cited Findings
- Cboe Market Intelligence "0DTEs Decoded" (2 May 2025): SPX 0DTE averages almost 2 million contracts per day, up more than five-fold over three years; retail estimated at about 50-60% of SPX 0DTE trading (fell from 57% to 47% during the early-April 2025 volatility spike, back to about 60% afterwards); more than 95% of 0DTE trades are limited-risk (long options or spreads), only about 4% naked short options; customer activity "extremely balanced"; net market maker gamma hedging "de minimis," at best 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity. No buyer/seller P&L given. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact), corroborated by [FOW](https://www.fow.com/insights/analysis-cboe-points-to-retail-flow-as-zero-day-options-grow) (0DTE a record 55% of SPX volume in Q1 2025; average volume up 29% y/y; put/call about 1)
- Cboe Market Intelligence "Crossed Wires: Separating Perception from Reality" (14 July 2026; reported by a crypto outlet, not the original): 0DTE over 60% of typical SPX options volume in Q2 2026; average daily notional peaked at $2.3 trillion; about 92% of SPX 0DTE activity uses defined-risk structures; compares a hypothetical 10x short perpetual future (+9.4%) with an ATM 0DTE put (+404%) on 21 April 2026 (S&P -0.94% intraday). The outlet flags that the report reads as favorable to Cboe's products and that the 404% is a single hypothetical day. — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/cboe-perpetual-futures-vs-0dte-options/) (secondary; I did not open Cboe's original)
- Independent cross-check on scale: SIFMA reports 0DTE rose from 18% of total options contract volume in Jan 2023 to almost 30% in March 2026 — [SIFMA April 2026 metrics](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/SIFMA-Insights-Equities-and-Options-Monthly-Metrics_April_2026.pdf) (search snippet only). Northern Trust says about 23% of US options flow in 2025 — [Northern Trust](https://www.northerntrust.com/united-states/insights-research/asset-servicing/a-suite/insights-hub/options-quarterly-commentary-q4-2025) (snippet only).
- Retail share definitions conflict: Cboe's retail share is 50-60% of SPX 0DTE; Beckmeyer et al. identify retail only via the price-improvement auction (orders of at most 10 contracts, from Feb 2021) and find retail is 6% or more of 0DTE and about 2-4% of all SPX volume, with the 75% figure being the share of retail's own SPX trades that are 0DTE. These measure different things and are not reconciled; neither is precise. — [Beckmeyer et al.](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf), [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)
- Beckmeyer et al. document Cboe's role: Cboe introduced daily expirations (16 May 2022) and AIM price improvement for SPX orders, with SIFMA support, to attract retail flow. — [Beckmeyer et al.](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cboe's "limited-risk structures" statistic is consistent with the academic finding that multi-leg trades are the better-performing category, but "limited-risk" is not "profitable"; Beckmeyer et al. show multi-leg retail also loses in aggregate (although its net losses plateaued from early 2023).
- Cboe's de minimis gamma claim (0.2%) is an interested-party estimate; it is partly consistent with independent academic papers in Q5 (no volatility amplification), so directionally plausible but not independent.

### Gaps
- No Cboe-published buyer/seller P&L found. I did not locate the original "Crossed Wires" PDF or Cboe's earlier 2023 0DTE notes; 2026 numbers are secondhand.
- Cboe's market-maker-versus-customer net position series (the Open/Close data used by Beckmeyer et al.) was not obtained.

## Q4. Academic evidence on intraday momentum / opening-range continuation in index ETFs. Effect sizes, robustness, recency, and whether dealer gamma explains it.

### Takeaway
The statistical evidence for market intraday momentum (early/rest-of-day return predicts last-30-minute return) is real but small (R-squared roughly 1-3%), mostly documented through 2013 (SPY) and 2020 (futures). The newer "opening-range breakout" and "noise-area" papers claim large Sharpe ratios, but they come from authors who sell research/trading education, the headline numbers depend on leverage and vol-scaling, and an independent replication finds the edge vanishes after realistic costs.

### Cited Findings

**Gao, Han, Li, Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum," Journal of Financial Economics (2018), 129, 394-414**
- SPY (and other ETFs) 1993-2013: the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return; stronger on high-volatility, high-volume, recession and macro-news days. — [ScienceDirect record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18301351) (via search result), [Substack summary](https://substack.com/@quantifiedstrategies/note/c-151224359)
- In-sample R-squared about 1.6%; out-of-sample R-squared 1.4% (first half-hour alone) and 2.0% (adding the 12th half-hour); an earlier draft (SPY 1999-2012) reported R-squared about 2% and OOS 1.7%/2.53%. The 12th-half-hour predictability largely comes from the financial-crisis period, while the first-half-hour effect is significant in crisis and non-crisis alike. — secondary summaries in search results, e.g. [Alpha Architect summary of the 2014 draft](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/); I did not read the JFE text.
- The effect is stronger when the first half-hour is positive than when negative — same secondary summaries.

**Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum," JFE 142 (2021) 377-403 (read in full)**
- 60+ futures (17 equity index, 16 bond, 21 commodity, 8 FX), Dec 1974 to May 2020; the return in the last 30 minutes is positively predicted by the return over the rest of the day (previous close to 30 minutes before close, "rROD"); predictability reverts over the next days; present in both the 1974-1999 and 2000-2020 subsamples. — [JFE paper PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- rROD is a better predictor than Gao et al.'s first-half-hour return: in the pooled equity-index futures table the first-half-hour predictor has a negative OOS R-squared of -1.71% (in-sample about 1.49%) while the rest-of-day predictor has positive OOS R-squared of about 2.9% (in-sample about 2.45%). The authors state their result "differs" from Gao et al. in that the first-half-hour predictor is weaker. (Numbers read from a layout-extracted regression table; treat as approximate.) — [same PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Strategy: a simple market-intraday-momentum strategy yields annualized Sharpe ratios 0.87-1.73 at the asset-class level (gross; the paper does not model transaction costs, but states that S&P 500 futures still show a positive net Sharpe with a one-tick cost). — [same PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Dealer-gamma link: they proxy the net gamma exposure (NGE) of S&P 500 option market makers (OptionMetrics open interest 1996-2017, then SqueezeMetrics data to May 2020; 2,930 negative-NGE days vs 3,158 positive). Intraday momentum is "much more pronounced" on negative-NGE days and insignificant on positive-NGE days. The paper also ties the effect to leveraged-ETF rebalancing. The proxy assumes customers buy puts and sell calls, so it is an inference, not observed dealer inventory; the authors note OTC positions are missing. — [same PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Follow-up: Baltussen, Da and Soebhag (2025) find a distinct "end-of-day reversal" in individual stocks not explained by liquidity or gamma-hedging (secondary). — [EUR summary](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/)

**Zarattini, Aziz, Barbon, "Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P500 ETF (SPY)" (SSRN 4824172; first version 10 May 2024; Feb 3 2025 version read; Swiss Finance Institute RP 24-97; working paper, not peer-reviewed; 4th place Quantpedia Awards 2025)**
- Strategy: trades SPY when price leaves a time-varying "noise area" (band from average intraday move over 14 days), with a VWAP-based trailing stop; May 2007 to April 2024; commission $0.0035/share and slippage $0.001/share (the latter from the authors' own small April 2024 live test). — [paper PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- Headline: total return 1,985%, annualized 19.6%, volatility 14.3%, Sharpe 1.33, max drawdown about 25%, daily hit ratio about 43%, beta slightly below 0 — but only after volatility-based position sizing with leverage capped at 4x. The unscaled base version made only 178% total (6.2%/yr, vol 10.9%, Sharpe 0.61), below buy-and-hold SPY, with worst daily return -10.33% and negative skew. — [same PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- Regimes: Sharpe about 1.5 for VIX above 6 and about 3.5 when VIX at the open exceeds 40 (declining at even higher VIX), i.e. the edge is concentrated in high-vol days. — [same PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- Gamma test: not an actual gamma measure. They regress strategy daily return on the prior-day 5-day RSI of SPY as a proxy for dealer gamma (rising market = dealers long gamma = mean reversion). Slope = -3.25 bps per RSI point, p = 0.001. — [same PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- Conflict: authors are affiliated with Concretum Research (sells research, code and tools) and Peak Capital Trading / Bear Bull Traders (day-trading education); the paper's FAQ directs readers to their code and email. Only Barbon is an academic (Univ. of St. Gallen). — [same PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content), [Concretum page](https://concretumgroup.com/beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-sp500-etf-spy/)

**Zarattini and Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable? Evidence of Sustainable Long-term Profits from Opening Range Breakout (ORB) Day Trading Strategies" (SSRN 4416622, 2023, QQQ)**
- 5-minute ORB on QQQ, 2016-2023: reported 1,484% for a 3x TQQQ version vs 169% for QQQ buy-and-hold, annualized alpha 33% net of commissions; commissions modelled but no spread or slippage; no out-of-sample period. — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622) (via search result), [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable/)
- Independent replication (blog, not peer reviewed): same rule on CFDs for NQ, SPX, Dow, DAX, FTSE, Jan 2015 to Jun 2026, about 2,900 sessions each. Gross reproduces for NQ (about +0.13 to +0.15 R per trade, 23% hit rate); with spread and slippage charged, net is +0.002 R for NQ and negative for SPX (-0.081 R), Dow (-0.081), DAX (-0.038), FTSE (-0.079); excess versus a random-direction benchmark is +0.124 R (t 2.1) for NQ and +0.092 R (t 1.2) for SPX; 2021-2026 out-of-sample at best +0.06 R on NQ; the author attributes the 33% alpha to zero spread/slippage, 4x leverage and a 2016-2023 window that skips weak years (2015-2017 negative in every market). The replicator sells MQL5 trading products. CFD costs are likely larger than SPY/QQQ ETF costs, so this may overstate cost drag for ETF execution. — [MQL5 replication blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)

### Inferences
- The robust academic fact is the small, state-dependent last-30-minutes continuation (R-squared 1-3%), strongest when dealers are net short gamma and on high-vol days. It is a closing-hour effect, not an open-to-close "buy the opening range break" effect.
- The ORB/noise-area results are not independent confirmation of the Gao/Baltussen effect: the signals are different, headline numbers use 3x-4x leverage, and the independent replication suggests most gross edge on index CFDs is about the size of round-trip costs. For SPY/QQQ ETF costs are lower, so a cost-adjusted edge is possible but unproven out of sample.
- Post-2013 SPY robustness of Gao's first-half-hour predictor is unproven: Baltussen's pooled futures results show a negative OOS R-squared for it, and the only recent SPY-specific evidence is the Zarattini (vol-scaled, leveraged) backtest, which is conflicted.
- For a 0DTE options developer: even a real 1-3% R-squared closing-hour effect would have to be monetized with options paying 5-6% effective spreads (SPX data) and theta decay; none of the evidence reviewed shows a net-of-cost options edge from these signals.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed post-2013 SPY/QQQ out-of-sample test of Gao et al. or Baltussen et al. was found. (A global replication, "Intraday time series momentum: global evidence," J. Financial Markets 2022, reports the effect in 12 of 16 markets per a search snippet; I did not read it: [Reading CentAUR](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf).)
- No source tested the signals using 0DTE option payoffs (as opposed to the underlying ETF/futures) net of option spreads.
- Zarattini's tables on daily patterns and day-of-week t-stats were not extracted.

## Q5. Dealer gamma exposure (GEX) and intraday volatility, mean reversion vs trending, and whether it is tradeable.

### Takeaway
Academic evidence supports a gamma-hedging channel: when dealers are net short gamma, intraday momentum and volatility rise; when net long, reversals and lower volatility. But dealer positions are inferred, not observed, effects decay within about an hour, and the most-cited 0DTE-specific results say 0DTE gamma lowers (or does not raise) volatility. No peer-reviewed evidence of a net-of-cost tradeable GEX strategy was found.

### Cited Findings
- Baltussen et al. (JFE 2021): momentum present only on negative-NGE days for S&P 500 (see Q4). — [JFE PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Barbon and Buraschi, "Gamma Fragility" (SSRN 3725454, working paper): large panel of index and equity options; aggregate gamma imbalance; intraday momentum (reversal) is explained by the interaction of negative (positive) ex-ante gamma imbalance and illiquidity; daily return volatility is negatively related to prior-day gamma imbalance; the effect is stronger for less liquid underlyings; also linked to flash-crash frequency and magnitude. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3725454) (via search summaries; paper not read). The weaker effect for liquid underlyings is a caution for SPX/SPY/QQQ.
- Dim, Eraker, Vilkov, "0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation" (SSRN 4692190, 2024): high 0DTE open-interest gamma does not propagate volatility; it is associated with lower realized intraday volatility; no propagation into overnight or lagged intraday volatility; intraday 0DTE volume shocks do not amplify recent index returns; the market response to 0DTE volume shocks differs between early and late sample by only 0.1 standard deviations of absolute returns. — [Oxford seminar abstract](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/node/67396), [Harbourfront Quant](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-0dte-options-cause-for) (secondary)
- Adams et al., "Do S&P500 Options Increase Market Volatility? Evidence from 0DTEs" (SSRN 5641974, Oct 2025): 0DTEs reduce index volatility by about 60 annualized bps on days with 0DTE trading (identified from the Tue/Thu schedule before May 2022); the effect comes from longer-dated options that roll into 0DTE status, not expiry-day trading; higher dealer net gamma predicts lower S&P 500 volatility over the next 10 minutes (fading after about 1 hour), stronger E-mini order-flow reversals, and negative high-frequency momentum; positive-gamma hedging can be profitable for market makers if transaction costs are low; 1 bp more hedging need is associated with about 4 bp less realized volatility. — [Quantpedia summary of the abstract](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/) (secondary)
- Brogaard, Han, Won (2023, "How Does Zero-Day-To-Expiry Options Trading Affect the Volatility of Underlying Assets?") — cited by Beckmeyer et al. as showing 0DTE trading impacts S&P 500 volatility; I did not obtain its findings. — [Beckmeyer et al. reference](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Cboe estimate: net market-maker gamma hedging at best 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact) (interested party)
- Zarattini et al. use a price-based proxy (5-day RSI) for the dealer-gamma regime and find a significant relationship with their momentum strategy's returns (-3.25 bps per RSI point, p=0.001); they state estimating gamma properly is beyond scope. — [paper PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)

### Inferences
- Taken together: positive dealer gamma is associated with mean reversion and lower short-horizon vol; negative with trending. That gives a regime-filter hypothesis (trend-following on negative-GEX days, fade or sell premium on positive-GEX days). Supportive evidence is the Baltussen NGE split and the Adams intraday results; horizons are minutes to about an hour, which matters for 0DTE option timing.
- Caveats against a retail GEX product: (a) dealer position sign is assumed (customers long puts, short calls), and Baltussen et al. note that OTC positions are missing; (b) Beckmeyer et al. show end customers net buy 0DTE, leaving dealers net short 0DTE gamma, which is opposite to the "dealers are long gamma, damping vol" assumption in many vendor GEX models, while Adams et al. find the damping arises from rolled-in older positions, so the sign of the effect in the 0DTE era is contested; (c) effects fade within about an hour and are strongest in less liquid underlyings; (d) no transaction-cost-adjusted GEX trading result found.
- Vendors (SpotGamma, SqueezeMetrics) are interested parties; I found no independent out-of-sample evaluation of commercial GEX signals. (Baltussen et al. thank SqueezeMetrics for data.)

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evidence that a GEX-based signal produces net-of-cost profits for retail on SPY/QQQ/SPX options.
- Dim-Eraker-Vilkov and Adams et al. come from abstracts or summaries; sample periods and t-stats not verified.
- No QQQ-specific GEX evidence (a Cboe/SpotGamma "cboeqqq" file appeared in search but was not opened and is vendor material).
