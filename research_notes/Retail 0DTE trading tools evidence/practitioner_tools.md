# Retail 0DTE automation tools, strategy families and published track records (SPX/SPY/QQQ, 2022-Oct 2026)

Sourcing notes for the report writer:
- Evidence grades: **[Independent]** = academic / non-vendor with stated method; **[Vendor-research]** = platform's own analysis of its user data (conflicted: sells bots/education); **[Vendor-claim]** = marketing / paid signal / unverifiable; **[Secondary]** = blog or aggregator relaying a primary I could not open.
- Several primary pages (tastylive episode pages, Option Alpha community posts, SSRN papers, CXO paywalled pieces) returned no body text to my fetcher. Where a figure comes only from a search-engine summary of such a page, I say "search summary"; treat those as unverified until the primary is opened.
- No audited, broker-statement-level track record of an automated retail 0DTE SPX/SPY/QQQ strategy over a multi-year period was found. This absence is itself a finding.

## 1. Which platforms/tools do retail traders use to automate 0DTE, and what do they typically run?

### Takeaway
Retail automation clusters in three layers: (a) no-code bot platforms with built-in 0DTE backtesters (Option Alpha, Option Omega), (b) broker-API/code stacks (QuantConnect, IBKR/TradeStation/Alpaca) used by developers, and (c) paid "signal"/data services (Tachyon-style signal vendors, QuantedOptions, SpotGamma). The dominant automated strategy is defined-risk premium selling (iron condors, iron butterflies, credit spreads) on SPX/SPY/QQQ entered in the morning to mid-day. Directional ORB/momentum and LLM agents exist mostly as papers, hackathon demos and marketing, not as audited live records.

### Cited Findings
- Option Alpha: commercial no-code bot platform; its 0DTE Oracle backtester runs one fixed setup (OTM %, reward/risk, width, entry time) across every day over 1-3 years using 1-minute options data (SPX, SPY, QQQ, IWM, XSP supported) and can generate a bot from a backtest. Its research page notes sample windows vary (180 days vs "last year") and that results are illustrative. — [Option Alpha: top 0DTE strategies](https://optionalpha.com/learn/top-0dte-options-strategies); platform description via search summary of [Option Alpha backtesting research](https://optionalpha.com/members/backtesting-research)
- Option Alpha live platform data: its own 2023 (updated Feb 2025) study covers ~230k 0DTE trades closed on the platform; SPY was 81% of trades, QQQ second, all other tickers under 6%; iron butterflies the most popular structure; neutral strategies (incl. iron condors) about twice as common as directional credit spreads. — [Option Alpha: 0DTE options strategy performance](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- Option Alpha bot template exists with FOMC/CPI/day-of-week skip switches and a VIX threshold (default 33) plus ADX/moving-average trend gating (search summary; backtest figures not seen). — [Option Alpha 0DTE iron condor w/ iron fly hedging template](https://optionalpha.com/bots/0dte-iron-condor-w-iron-fly-hedging)
- Option Omega: backtester with 1-minute bid/ask history back to 2013; its tutorial example is an SPX 0DTE iron condor, entry 10:00 ET, 10-delta shorts, long wings 25 points out, 25% profit target, 2x-credit stop, time exit 15:50. The tutorial gives no results, test period, slippage or fill assumptions and calls it a hypothesis to test, not a recommendation. — [Option Omega how-to](https://optionomega.com/blog/how-to-backtest-a-0dte-iron-condor-on-spx/)
- Option Omega community example, "Maria shoe trade": SPX ATM iron fly with $20 wings, entered ~10:30 ET, 25% of credit profit target, exit by 14:30; credit ~ $1,200, risk ~ $800 per contract; claimed live average ~ $111/day/contract since mid-2023 (self-reported, no statements shown, no win rate or drawdown). Reader comments in the same article report a backtest from Jan 2023 with fees ending at -$9,581 from $10k, and another reader losing $350 in two weeks of paper trading. — [Theta Profits: Maria shoe trade](https://www.thetaprofits.com/the-maria-shoe-trade-how-a-0dte-iron-butterfly-paid-for-1500-shoes-again-and-again/)
- Dale Perryman (via Theta Profits, Oct 2026): signal when the opening expected move decays to 83% of its opening value; wings start ~50 points wide and narrow through the day; exit when SPX moves from the short strike by an amount equal to the credit collected; avoids third Wednesday, last trading day of month and FOMC days. Reports (unverified) >$4M cumulative group profit, ~ $800k max drawdown, ~30% annual returns, worst single day ~ -$11k/contract; flies are 25-30% of allocation but 75-80% of return. — [Theta Profits: Dale Perryman](https://www.thetaprofits.com/0dte-iron-flies-dale-perryman-on-4m-in-profits-and-an-800k-drawdown/)
- Cboe's own example condor (Henry Schwartz, HOOD Summit 2025): SPX 10-point-wide put and call spreads sold mid-day (~12:49 ET) for about $1.00 each side after a volatility spike, set-and-forget with sizing instead of stops, optional 10-cent standing bid. No win rate or track record given; the example is a single trade (+$200). — [Cboe: Schwartz zero-day iron condor](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/henry-schwartzs-zero-day-spx-iron-condor-strategy-a-deep-dive/)
- Cboe: >95% of SPX 0DTE trades use limited-risk formats (long options or spreads); only ~4% is naked short options; retail estimated ~50-60% of SPX 0DTE volume (May 2025). — [Cboe: 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact/)
- QuantConnect: forum threads show recurring data/engineering problems for SPX 0DTE backtests (missing chains with a DTE=0 filter, stale fills, minute vs hourly resolution mix-ups); a commenter advises against stop-losses on short single options because option prices are more volatile than the index; the poster moved to spreads. No verified strategy performance in the threads reviewed (search summary). — [QuantConnect forum 12111](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/12111), [QuantConnect forum 14857](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/14857/anyone-know-how-to-better-sell-dte-0-spxw/)
- LLM/AI options agents: only hackathon-grade projects found (Alpaca AI Trading Agents hackathon on lablab.ai). Pattern: a deterministic engine picks strikes and sizes; the LLM is limited to commentary or VETO/SIZE_DOWN/APPROVE gating on SPY 0DTE verticals/condors, run on Alpaca paper. No published P&L found. — [lablab.ai EasoLab Alpaca agent](https://lablab.ai/submissions/jnj5lhf3bm7jz19u796yp5ct), [lablab.ai FLINCH](https://lablab.ai/ai-hackathons/alpaca-ai-trading-agents-hackathon/convex/flinch) (search summaries; not opened)
- General LLM-trading research (stocks, not options): FINSABER (two decades, 100+ symbols) reports that LLM-strategy advantages in earlier work shrink considerably with longer windows and wider universes, attributing inflated results to survivorship/data-snooping bias; StockBench (Mar-Jul 2025, daily buy/sell/hold) finds most LLM agents struggle to beat buy-and-hold. — [FINSABER arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078), [StockBench arXiv 2510.02209](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.02209) (search summaries)
- Open-source GitHub: search found general LLM stock-trading backtesters (e.g. prakhxr0/quant, jason8745/llm-agent-trader, Open-Finance-Lab/AgenticTrading) but no maintained open-source project combining LLM + 0DTE options + realistic backtest. — [prakhxr0/quant](https://github.com/prakhxr0/quant)
- Free paper-trading simulator 0dtespx.com offers 0DTE SPX practice/backtest with configurable fees ($1.72 open / $0.72 close per contract, $5 per strike at settlement) and optional slippage; operator unnamed. — [0dtespx.com](https://0dtespx.com/)

### Inferences
- Execution tooling is mature (bots, 1-minute bid/ask backtesters) but validation is weak: none of the platforms reviewed publishes a net-of-costs, out-of-sample, audited result.
- Defined-risk structures dominate partly for margin/broker reasons and partly tail-risk fear; this matches Option Alpha's SPY-heavy, iron-fly-heavy user base.

### Gaps
- No TradeStation/IBKR-specific 0DTE bot repos with results were found.
- No evidence on how many QuantConnect-hosted 0DTE strategies have live results.

## 2. Strategy families and reported performance

### Takeaway
Premium-selling (condors/flies) shows high headline win rates (~63-80%), but that is structural (small credit vs larger max loss) and says little about expectancy. Independent academic work finds the 0DTE variance risk premium real but too small to survive transaction costs, with realised skewness driving P&L. Published net-positive results come from vendors, mid-price backtests, or self-reports. Directional ORB and GEX strategies have no independently verified options-based 0DTE track record.

### Cited Findings

**Short premium (iron condors / flies / credit spreads)**
- [Vendor-research] Option Alpha, ~230k live trades: 49.64% of traders using a 0DTE strategy were profitable; among traders with >100 trades who were net profitable (~20% of traders), iron butterflies won 66.76% and iron condors 70.19% (a survivorship-conditioned subsample); profitable traders entered ~10:15 ET and exited shortly after 12:00 (average hold ~2 hours); Monday most profitable, Tuesday/Thursday/Friday net negative overall. Study text gives no average P&L per trade (charts only) and treats trades as independent. — [Option Alpha: 0DTE options strategy performance](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance); 49.64% also in search summary of [Option Alpha research insights bot](https://optionalpha.com/bots/0dte-research-insights-bot)
- [Vendor-research] Another Option Alpha cut (search summary): 4,959 iron butterflies with 72% win rate and 3,170 iron condors with 63%; and a stats page listing iron condor 65.06% / iron butterfly 65% across all live trades. Different samples, not reconciled. — [Option Alpha 0DTE blog](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte)
- [Vendor-research] Option Alpha SMA5 contrarian setups (short call spread on SMA5 buy signal, short put spread on SMA5 sell signal): profit factor >2.0, win rate >75%, ~17% of trades; portfolio simulation of 37,894 trades over ~3 years treating trades as independent, final P&L not stated in text. — [Option Alpha: 0DTE options strategy performance](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- [Vendor-research] Option Alpha "peg" research (SPX, 180 days to Aug 2024): SPX closed within 0.2% of its 14:00 price 65.6% of the time and within 0.3% of its 10:30 price 53.3%; a 0.3%-OTM iron condor entered 13:00 realised max profit 66.1% of the time. Worked example: 0.2% OTM, $5-wide, 14:44 entry, credit $196, risk $304, ~68% max-profit rate, simplified EV ~ +$36/trade (the article's own calc, ignoring partial outcomes, fees and slippage). — [Option Alpha: top 0DTE strategies](https://optionalpha.com/learn/top-0dte-options-strategies)
- [Vendor-research, unverified] tastylive iron-fly study (Aug 2025, search summary of the episode; page body not retrievable): ~2 years of SPX 0DTE flies; $20 wings with 10% profit target 78% win rate, 20% target 61% win but larger average win ($177 vs $92). **Conflicts with** a community thread describing the same tastylive video as 70% win / $61 average for $10 wings and a 10% target, where the replicator got 55% win / -$8 average on Option Alpha's 1-minute backtester (commenters blame tastylive's 10-minute data and mid-price fills with no slippage). The two descriptions differ on wing width, so they may be different cuts of the study. — [tastylive Market Measures episode](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/zero-dte-winners-and-losers-for-iron-flies-08-07-2025); [Option Alpha community thread](https://app.optionalpha.com/community/posts/0-dte-backtest-tasty-gives-completely--202508077174) (both via search summaries)
- [Vendor-research, unverified] tastylive 0DTE iron condor management (Mar 2026 episode, search summary): 72 management variations tested; ~25% profit target gave the best balance; win rates barely changed across scenarios. — [tastylive: legging out of 0DTE iron condors part 2](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/0-dte-breakdown-legging-out-in-0-dte-iron-condors-part-2-03-30-2026)
- [Secondary] Stop-loss study attributed to tastytrade (blog with no link to primary, possible AI-assisted writing): SPX 0DTE delta-20 iron condor, $20 wings, from 20 May 2022; avg credit ~ $500, max loss ~ $1,500; unmanaged win rate ~72%; 2x-credit stop below 70%, 1x below 60%, 0.5x below 50%; ~48% of positions stopped at 0.5x would have recovered to breakeven by expiry; ~60% of 0DTE trades had an intraday loss of at least 50% of credit before recovering; average P&L stayed positive at all stop levels (no dollar figures); return on capital roughly halved at 0.5x. — [StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/?p=47372)
- [Self-reported] Dale Perryman iron flies: ~30% annual returns, ~$800k max drawdown vs >$4M cumulative profit, worst day ~ -$11k per contract; no win rate; unaudited. — [Theta Profits](https://www.thetaprofits.com/0dte-iron-flies-dale-perryman-on-4m-in-profits-and-an-800k-drawdown/)

**Straddles/strangles and all-structure hold-to-expiry**
- [Independent] Vilkov, "0DTE Trading Rules" (SSRN 4641356): SPX 0DTE strategies established daily and held to expiry, Sep 2016 - Jan 11 2024 (earlier draft through May 2023 using 30-minute prices; entries 10:00 main, plus 13:00 and 15:00). Findings: a statistically significant 0DTE variance risk premium; individual option returns very volatile and skewed; mean returns of most strategies not significant; at the median some strategies (e.g. buying deep ITM calls plus selling OTM calls/puts) can be profitable, while in the Nov 2023 draft most popular structures (strangles, bull/bear spreads, ratio spreads) had negative median returns with risk reversal the exception; realised index skewness explains most P&L, so any edge needs a conditional rule. — [Hull Tactical summary](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/), [CXO Advisory (paywalled; sample details only)](https://cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/0dte-strategy-outcomes)
- [Secondary] A blog read of the same paper: VRP ~ 0.001% from 10:00 to close, "statistically real, economically irrelevant", transaction costs swallow it; risk reversal mean ~ 0.01% of index value. These numbers come only from the blog and are not verified against the paper. — [Breaking NASDAQ](https://breakingnasdaq.beehiiv.com/p/the-0dte-illusion)
- [Independent] Chong (HKUST), SPX 0DTE 2020-2024: large risk premia in 0DTE prices, but after transaction costs and estimation risk, volatility-arbitrage Sharpe ratios are economically insignificant; "expensive but do not appear to be mispriced". — [HKUST Business Insight](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2026/04/are-0dte-options-mispriced)
- [Secondary] Substack simulation (~2.5 years SPX): selling one-day ATM straddles lost slightly on average; a CAIA piece notes that selling negative-edge straddles shows profits most days until the tail loss arrives, so win rate misleads. — [The Till](https://thetill.substack.com/p/the-pot-of-gold-is-the-trades-we) (search summary)
- [Vendor-research] Execution cost (NDX 0DTE, Volos): assuming 25% mid-to-bid slippage, daily entry and exit cost ~300-350 bps per year; exiting expiring ITM options alone cost >350 bps/year (ITM expiring spread ~6 bps, more than double the full-sample average); letting ITM options cash-settle would cut roll-out costs >50%. — [Volos Software](https://volossoftware.com/insights/improving-0dte-trading-returns)

**Retail aggregate outcomes**
- [Independent] Beckmeyer, Branger, Gayda (SSRN 4404704): Cboe trade data Jan 2021 - Feb 2023; retail lost >$70M over a little over two years; average daily retail loss ~ $184k over the whole sample and ~ $358k/day after daily expirations began 16 May 2022; a summary (author unnamed, search level) says >$50M of the loss was transaction costs and that gross-of-fee (mid-quote) profits were already negative. Neudata cites ~ $125M aggregate, so totals vary by source. — [Bloomberg via BNN](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634), [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance), [Neudata](https://neudata.co/literature-reviews/zero-day-to-expiry-options-a-losing-bet-for-retail-traders)
- Retail share conflict: academic/JPMorgan estimates ~3-5% (authors' 3-4% of S&P options volume; JPMorgan 5% of 0DTE) vs Cboe's ~50-60% of SPX 0DTE volume (May 2025; different classification, vendor with product interest). — [Neudata](https://neudata.co/literature-reviews/zero-day-to-expiry-options-a-losing-bet-for-retail-traders), [Cboe 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact/)

**Directional / ORB / momentum**
- Zarattini & Aziz (SSRN 4416622) ORB on QQQ **shares**, not options: trade direction of the first 5-minute candle, stop at the opposite side; ~24% hit rate, +0.13 R per trade, ~33% annualised at 4x leverage; commissions only, no spread/slippage, no out-of-sample. — as described in [MQL5 replication post](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- [Independent but non-peer-reviewed] Replication by an individual trader on index CFDs (NQ, SPX, Dow, DAX, FTSE), Jan 2015 - Jun 2026, ~2,900 sessions each, unoptimised rule: gross NQ +0.131 R matches the paper (23.2% hit rate vs 24%); after spread/slippage (NQ 2.5 pts, SPX 0.8, ~6-12% of risk) no market is statistically different from zero and four of five are negative (SPX net -0.081 R, t = -1.6); negative in all markets in 2015-2017; 2021-2026 at best +0.06 R on NQ; first-candle direction beats random direction by only ~0.1 R, about the size of round-trip cost. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- [Authors' own claim] Zarattini/Aziz/Barbon "Beat the Market" (Concretum) on SPY shares, 1-minute data May 2007 - Apr 2024: ~19.6% annualised, Sharpe 1.33, total return 1,985% "net of costs". Shares, not options. — [MoneyShow listing](https://moneyshow.com/video/bbcd45cf3c41464aa98444de4aa095f41) (search summary)
- I found no published 0DTE long-option ORB track record with realistic bid/ask fills; the Option Alpha ORB article and a "BaronOpsBot" PDF are promotional or summary-level. — [Option Alpha ORB article](https://optionalpha.com/blog/opening-range-breakout-0dte-options-trading-strategy-explained)

**GEX / dealer gamma**
- [Vendor-research, Cboe] Median net market-maker SPX 0DTE gamma at 15:30 only about +$173M (IQR -$1.1B to +$2.4B), equal to 1.3-1.9% of S&P futures daily notional at the whiskers; in May 2025 Cboe says net MM gamma hedging is de minimis (at best 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity) and customer flow "extremely balanced". Scope is SPX 0DTE only (ignores SPY and other expiries). — [Cboe: Much Ado About 0DTEs](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options), [Cboe: 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact/)
- SpotGamma publishes framework guidance (call wall, put wall, gamma flip; mean reversion in positive gamma, larger swings in negative gamma) but no performance numbers; it acknowledges dealer gamma is estimated from open interest using sign conventions and that OI is stale intraday. — [SpotGamma 0DTE](https://spotgamma.com/0dte) (search summary)
- No independent academic out-of-sample test of GEX-based trading rules was found. — search: "gamma exposure GEX independent academic test" returned only vendor material

### Inferences
- High win rate + small credit + larger max loss is arithmetic, not edge: Cboe's example risks $9 to make $1; stop-loss data show win rate collapsing as stops tighten.
- Vilkov, Chong and Beckmeyer et al. all point the same direction: gross edge near zero, costs decide. This conflicts with vendor mid-price backtests showing positive EV.
- Whatever edge exists is conditional (skewness prediction, event filters), which is where retail overfitting risk is highest.

### Gaps
- tastylive primary tables (win rate / avg P&L / drawdown) not retrievable; the 78% and $177/$92 figures are single-source and conflict with a second description (70% / $61).
- Vilkov's actual median/mean/t-stat tables and cost assumptions not seen (SSRN inaccessible).
- No numbers found for retail long straddle/strangle performance beyond aggregate losses.
- No drawdown statistics from Option Alpha research (charts only).

## 3. Backtest vs live: how much do published 0DTE backtests decay?

### Takeaway
No systematic study of backtest-to-live decay for retail 0DTE exists in the sources found. Indirect evidence is consistent: results flip with fill assumptions and data resolution (a replication of a tastylive iron-fly study went from a claimed +$61 average to -$8 on 1-minute data), edge faded after daily SPY/QQQ expirations arrived, and only ~50% of platform users are profitable.

### Cited Findings
- Replication gap: a community member replicating a tastylive iron-fly (10% target) backtest on Option Alpha's 1-minute data got 55% win / -$8 average vs the tastylive-claimed ~70% / +$61; commenters attribute the gap to 10-minute data and mid-price fills without slippage. — [Option Alpha community thread](https://app.optionalpha.com/community/posts/0-dte-backtest-tasty-gives-completely--202508077174) (search summary; page not directly readable)
- Regime decay: Option Alpha says late-2022 daily expirations in SPY and QQQ slowed 0DTE activity and profits; a Reddit user analysing Oracle output said 2022 was better than 2023 and 2024 about half as productive for most strategies, so a 3-year backtest is regime-dependent. — [Option Alpha: 0DTE options strategy performance](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance); Reddit remark via search summary of [Option Alpha backtesting research](https://optionalpha.com/members/backtesting-research)
- Live platform aggregate: only 49.64% of Option Alpha 0DTE traders are profitable and Tue/Thu/Fri trades are net negative overall despite 63-72% iron fly/condor win rates. — [Option Alpha: 0DTE options strategy performance](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- Live-execution failure mode: one user's iron-fly bot built from a backtest struggled to close trades live and held positions for days while the paper-trading control behaved as expected; the user shut it down (single anecdote, search summary). — [Option Alpha community](https://optionalpha.com/bots/0dte-research-insights-bot)
- Slippage in the wild: a 1DTE SPX trader's early live log found 0.15 slippage adequate vs 0.2 assumed in backtests (single trader). — [Developing Trust in 1 DTE SPX Options](https://gvb3.substack.com/p/developing-trust-in-1-dte-spx-options) (search summary)
- Cost scale: ~300-350 bps/year of notional for daily NDX 0DTE entry and exit under 25% mid-to-bid slippage. — [Volos Software](https://volossoftware.com/insights/improving-0dte-trading-returns)
- ORB decay once costs applied: paper's ~33% annual QQQ return (commission-only, 4x leverage) vs replication net of spread/slippage ~zero or negative on index CFDs. — [MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- Vendor Tachyon: backtest (May 2022 - Jul 2026, 513 trades, 78.9% win, +$21,808 per 1 lot, Sharpe 2.32) omits slippage by its own disclaimer; live record since late May 2026 is "days, not years". — [Tachyon](https://futures.aeromir.com/tachyon)
- Tooling limits: Numerix white paper argues low-frequency backtests cannot capture intraday 0DTE dynamics and a small timing error can flip a trade from profit to loss (search summary). — [Numerix 0DTE strategies white paper](https://www.numerix.com/sites/default/files/file/2026-01/Numerix_White_Paper_0DTE_Trading_Strategies.pdf)

### Inferences
- A defensible numeric decay estimate cannot be derived. Qualitatively, the one worked replication flipped a positive average to slightly negative when moving to 1-minute bid/ask data, and annual round-trip cost (~3% of notional in the NDX example) is comparable in magnitude to the entire measured VRP.
- Reasonable prior: any published positive 0DTE backtest that omits slippage should be assumed near zero or negative net until live-tested.

### Gaps
- No audited live-vs-backtest comparison for any named 0DTE strategy; no Option Alpha publication comparing bots' backtests to their live clones; no follow-up of the Zarattini ORB papers in options.
- Could not retrieve r/thetagang or r/options threads with verifiable statements; none surfaced via search.

## 4. What reputable practitioners say works (entry time, stops/targets, delta/width, VIX/IV filters, events)

### Takeaway
Practitioner guidance converges on: enter after the opening volatility (about 10:00-10:30 ET, or mid-day after a spike), take profit early (10-25% of credit), be wary of tight stops, use defined risk and small size, and skip FOMC/CPI and similar event days. None of it is shown net-of-costs on independent data.

### Cited Findings
- Entry time: Option Alpha profitable traders enter ~10:15 ET and exit just after 12:00; its example iron-fly rule is entry 10:15, +15% target, -25% stop, time exit 12:00, Monday and Wednesday. — [Option Alpha 0DTE performance](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- Entry time variety: Option Alpha morning (10:30) vs afternoon (14:30) condor backtest shows a ~$15,000 P/L difference (details not seen); Schwartz/Cboe mid-day (~12:49); Maria 10:30; Option Omega example 10:00; 0dtespx.com example 09:35; Tachyon 15:51. — [Option Alpha video](https://optionalpha.com/videos/morning-vs-afternoon-0dte-backtested-options-strategies) (search summary), [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/henry-schwartzs-zero-day-spx-iron-condor-strategy-a-deep-dive/), [Tachyon](https://futures.aeromir.com/tachyon), [0dtespx.com](https://0dtespx.com/)
- Profit targets: tastylive (search summary) ~25% of credit best for 0DTE condors across 72 variants; for flies, a lower target (10%) gave a higher win rate but smaller average win. Maria 25%; Option Omega example 25% with 2x stop; 0dtespx.com example 50%. — [tastylive Mar 2026 episode](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/0-dte-breakdown-legging-out-in-0-dte-iron-condors-part-2-03-30-2026), [Option Omega](https://optionomega.com/blog/how-to-backtest-a-0dte-iron-condor-on-spx/)
- Stops: tastylive's older 45-DTE SPY research found managing losers at 1x-5x credit underperformed holding, and managing winners at 50% lifted win rate from 53% to 62% with slightly lower average P&L ($9.95 vs $11.57). For 0DTE, the secondary stop study found tight stops push win rate below 50% and ~48% of stopped trades would have recovered. Cboe's Schwartz uses no stops, only sizing; Perryman exits at a move equal to credit collected. — [tastylive managing losers](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/managing-losers-in-risk-defined-strategies-10-24-2016) (search summary), [StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/?p=47372), [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/henry-schwartzs-zero-day-spx-iron-condor-strategy-a-deep-dive/)
- Delta/width in use (no optimal found): 10-delta / 25-pt wings, 14-delta / 35-pt wings (Option Omega); 16-delta (0dtespx.com example); delta-20 / $20 wings (tastytrade study per secondary); 0.2-0.3% OTM $5-wide (Option Alpha); ATM fly $20 wings (Maria); 10-pt wide ~$1 credit (Cboe); 0.25-delta $5-wide (Tachyon). — links as above
- Event/VIX filters: Cboe's Schwartz avoids the Fed-announcement day, days before major data releases and extreme-uncertainty periods, and considers entries after the 08:30 data print; Perryman avoids FOMC, third Wednesday and month-end; the Option Alpha template offers FOMC/CPI/weekday switches and VIX<33. No published backtest quantifying these filters was found. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/henry-schwartzs-zero-day-spx-iron-condor-strategy-a-deep-dive/), [Theta Profits](https://www.thetaprofits.com/0dte-iron-flies-dale-perryman-on-4m-in-profits-and-an-800k-drawdown/), [Option Alpha template](https://optionalpha.com/bots/0dte-iron-condor-w-iron-fly-hedging)
- Sizing: Perryman won't trade below $50k and prefers $100k+; a secondary source cites 1-2% risk per trade and a 3% daily loss cap (unverified); Option Alpha found no discernible relation between dollars risked and performance. — [Theta Profits](https://www.thetaprofits.com/0dte-iron-flies-dale-perryman-on-4m-in-profits-and-an-800k-drawdown/), [StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/?p=47372), [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- Settlement: SPX cash-settles to the SET value, which can differ from the 16:00 screen price; matters for hold-to-expiry condors. — [Tachyon](https://futures.aeromir.com/tachyon)
- Volatility stress: in April 2025 spike Cboe saw retail's 0DTE share drop from 57% to 47%, then rebound to ~60%. — [Cboe 0DTEs Decoded](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact/)
- Spreads widen into the close, yet retail kept trading with under an hour to expiry. — [Bloomberg via BNN](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634)

### Inferences
- The "10:00-10:30" entry consensus is convention, not demonstrated optimum; sources disagree (09:35 to 15:51), and the only data shown (Option Alpha peg research) is a short window.
- Best-supported practice from the independent literature is risk control (defined risk, small size) and avoiding event-day gaps, not a specific entry time or delta.

### Gaps
- No numeric VIX-level or event-day filter results from tastylive, Option Alpha, SpotGamma or Cboe.
- No SpotGamma performance data.

## 5. Which claims are clearly marketing, and why

### Takeaway
Treat as marketing any claim with a win rate >75% quoted alone, a backtest that omits slippage, a live record of days, or paid access to the signal. The CFTC lists high or guaranteed returns from trading bots as red flags. Even useful platform research (Option Alpha, tastylive) is conflicted because the sponsor sells bots, education or data.

### Cited Findings
- Tachyon (paid SPX 0DTE condor signal): 78.9% win rate, profit factor 1.89, Sharpe 2.32, 13x profit/drawdown, "100% probability of profit" in Monte Carlo, 100% of 12-month rolling windows profitable. Red flags from its own page: no third-party audit or downloadable trade file; backtest omits slippage, liquidity limits and execution delay (own disclaimer); live record since 29 May 2026 is one author trade plus one subscriber screenshot; filters (no Mondays, momentum, ZLO>1.80, minimum credit $0.90) fitted on the same backtest; avg credit $1.26 on a $5-wide condor means ~ $3.74 risk per $1.26 collected, so a high win rate is expected; internal date/time inconsistencies. — [Tachyon](https://futures.aeromir.com/tachyon)
- QuantedOptions (subscription $39-$399/month): marketing cites a best win rate of 84.6% across 1,540 configurations (7 strategy types x 22 entry windows, 476 days, from Jan 2024; search summary). Picking the best of 1,540 is a multiple-testing artifact and win rate is not profit. Homepage shows no performance stats, no operator identity and no risk disclaimer in the extracted text. — [QuantedOptions](https://www.quantedoptions.com)
- 0dtespx.com: displays +41.8% return, Sharpe 1.72, 68% win with no stated strategy or period (it is a free simulator). — [0dtespx.com](https://0dtespx.com/)
- Option Alpha (sells subscriptions): research is real platform data but conflicted; it reports 66.76% / 70.19% win rates only for the profitable-trader subsample and 49.64% profitable overall, and states backtesting has limitations. — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)
- Theta Profits interviews (Perryman, Maria): self-reported profits and drawdown, no brokerage statements; reader comments in the Maria article report net-negative backtests and paper trading. — [Theta Profits Perryman](https://www.thetaprofits.com/0dte-iron-flies-dale-perryman-on-4m-in-profits-and-an-800k-drawdown/), [Maria shoe trade](https://www.thetaprofits.com/the-maria-shoe-trade-how-a-0dte-iron-butterfly-paid-for-1500-shoes-again-and-again/)
- CFTC advisory (Jan 2024), "AI Won't Turn Trading Bots into Money Machines": claims of high or guaranteed returns from bots and algorithms are red flags; the SEC sued a Texas man over fake AI trading bots promising 40-50% returns in 30-45 days (crypto; conduct Oct 2022 - mid-2024). — [CFTC release 8854-24](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8854-24), [Cointelegraph on SEC case](https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-charges-texas-man-with-123m-crypto-fraud-fake-ai-trading-bots)
- Why a high win rate is not evidence: Cboe's example condor risks $9 to make $1; selling negative-edge straddles wins most days until the tail loss (CAIA commentary); Vilkov finds rare large reversals dominate P&L. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/henry-schwartzs-zero-day-spx-iron-condor-strategy-a-deep-dive/), [Breaking NASDAQ](https://breakingnasdaq.beehiiv.com/p/the-0dte-illusion)
- Source-quality warning: several "studies" appear only in aggregator or AI-style blogs (StockWireX, Breaking NASDAQ) that cite tastytrade/Frankfurt figures without links.

### Inferences
- Credibility checklist for the report: (1) net of bid/ask slippage? (2) data resolution (1-min vs 10-min vs EOD)? (3) number of configurations searched? (4) live record of at least a year with broker statements? (5) does the author sell the signal or bot? Every vendor item above fails at least three.
- Platform research is the most informative retail-facing data but is conflicted and shows roughly coin-flip profitability for real users.

### Gaps
- Could not read tastylive's primary studies, so I cannot confirm whether they disclose slippage beyond the community's claim of mid-price fills.
- No performance evidence for any named "AI/LLM 0DTE bot" service beyond hackathon projects; no independent audit of any 0DTE signal service.
- No Reddit posts with broker-statement-verified annual 0DTE P&L surfaced.
